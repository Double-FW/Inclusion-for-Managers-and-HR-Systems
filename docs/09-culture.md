## Practical awareness and respectful support

<a id="everyday-culture"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-CUL-001 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** Inclusion is reinforced through everyday behaviour and accessible learning.

Provide accessible, role-specific training in disability, Deaf awareness, neurodivergence, privacy, communication and responding to barriers. Use practical situations and refresh learning through normal work. Do not identify staff as teaching examples or require disclosure. Include paid lived-experience expertise with clear choice over participation and attribution.

Offer peer mentoring and networks without assuming a person wants a mentor with the same diagnosis. Protect time, resources and access. Build a culture where clarification, pauses and differing work methods are normal. Do not tell someone they are masking or insist that visible comfort proves psychological safety.

Sign-language learning or social exercises can be optional shared learning, but do not replace communication access. If an activity excludes a colleague, redesign it with their input or offer an equally valued alternative. Avoid activities that require personal health disclosure or personality labels.

**Boundary:** Training, networks and voluntary inclusion schemes support capability but do not prove compliance or replace operational change.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-17](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-17).

## Make development and progression equitable

<a id="progression"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-CUL-002 · **Action owner:** HR

**Intended outcome:** People can grow, earn and lead without having to conform to one career style.

Review access to stretch assignments, learning, mentoring, sponsorship, performance evidence and promotion panels. Publish criteria that reflect the work. Offer technical, specialist and other contribution routes alongside people management where the organisation supports them; do not assume every expert should manage staff to advance.

Check whether flexible or part-time staff miss informal opportunities, whether access failures reduce visible output, and whether disabled colleagues are steered away from ambitious work “for their own good”. Discuss aspirations and support with the person. A spiky skill profile is not a universal neurodivergent characteristic or a reason to confine someone to a narrow role.

Apply the same accessible assessment principles used in recruitment. Include organisational change, redeployment, retirement and exit: use clear information, accessible consultation and a fair review route. Invite voluntary exit feedback without pressuring people to give a diagnosis or attributing every departure to inclusion.

**Boundary:** Career paths must be resourced and published; do not promise a promotion route that has not been adopted.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Solve shared problems without exposing a colleague

<a id="team-solutions"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-CUL-003 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** Teams can improve a process while respecting choice and independence.

Ask privately whether the affected person wants team input and what may be shared. Frame the challenge around the process, goal or activity. Explore alternatives, including changes that initially seem unfamiliar, and test whether they work. Do not make the colleague's disability the team-building puzzle.

Buddying can be useful if freely chosen, but check privacy, status, workload and the ability to decline. Keep a route to independent participation; assistance must not become the permanent default because redesign feels inconvenient. Check that the chosen solution does not create a barrier for somebody else.

Document what changed, what was tested and what remained difficult. Share process learning with appropriate permission and without identifiable personal detail. Champions can help spread practical learning with allocated time and leadership backing; owners remain responsible for delivery and resources.

**Boundary:** Permission to try a team solution is not permission to publish a personal story or medical information.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-17](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-17).
