## What inclusion means

<a id="meaning"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-FND-001 · **Action owner:** executive sponsor

**Intended outcome:** People can participate, influence decisions and progress with dignity.

Inclusion is the continuing work of making opportunity and participation real. Someone can be employed yet excluded from information, decisions, informal networks, benefits or promotion. Ask what the person can do, influence and access, and what avoidable effort is required. An invitation or supportive intention is not enough.

Accessibility removes barriers in tools, information, environments and processes. Equality concerns rights and fair opportunity; equity recognises that different barriers may require different responses. Belonging is feeling accepted without having to perform a preferred personality. These ideas reinforce one another, but a training certificate or accessibility checklist does not demonstrate them all.

Use autonomy, effort, timeliness, confidence, satisfaction, safety and progression as questions in design and review. A comparable outcome can use a different method. Nobody should have to disclose a diagnosis or mimic colleagues' social style to be respected.

**Boundary:** Do not claim that identical conditions, identical speed or legal conformance alone establish inclusion.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-03](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-03).

## Individuals and intersecting barriers

<a id="individuals"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-FND-002 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** Support follows the person and their work rather than a diagnostic stereotype.

Ask what helps with this task in this setting, and what the person wants to try. Disabled, Deaf and neurodivergent identities overlap but are not interchangeable. People with the same diagnosis may have different needs; someone may not identify as disabled or may not have a diagnosis. Ask about identity-first or person-first language when relevant and respect the answer without repeatedly requiring explanation.

Consider fluctuating pain, fatigue, medication effects, time of day, changing environments and temporary barriers. Also consider how race, religion, sex, gender, age, caring, language, income and employment status affect access to support or safety in raising a concern. Do not infer that every difficulty is medical or that situational barriers reproduce every aspect of disability.

Offer a private conversation and an alternative contact if the manager is implicated. If work appears unsustainable, describe the situation and offer support; do not announce that you have detected masking or burnout.

**Boundary:** No diagnosis, strength profile, masking judgement or limitation may be inferred from behaviour or identity.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Working glossary and shared responsibility

<a id="glossary"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-FND-003 · **Action owner:** HR

**Intended outcome:** People can use the guidance without specialist vocabulary.

An adjustment is an agreed change to reduce a barrier; a reasonable adjustment has a specific legal context explained in the legal module. Universal provision means an accessible default available without individual negotiation. AAC means augmentative and alternative communication, including typed and device-mediated communication. Assistive technology includes screen readers, magnification, alternative input and other support; it depends on compatible systems and cannot repair every defect.

Job crafting means collaboratively shaping tasks or methods around work outcomes and the person's choices. A workplace passport is an optional confidential record of functional support, not a public profile. Masking describes concealment or suppression of traits or needs; it is not a management assessment category. Psychological safety concerns the ability to raise concerns without fear, rather than a promise that one purchase will eliminate distress.

Managers coordinate action; process, IT, procurement and facilities owners resolve the parts they control. Employees inform choices but do not carry responsibility for making the organisation accessible.

**Boundary:** Employee networks and champions support action; they do not replace accountable owners or legal duties.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-17](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-17).
