## Make the first day workable

<a id="ready-to-start"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-ONB-001 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** People begin with usable tools, information and support.

Confirm the start arrangements privately before the person arrives: building access or remote setup, accessible induction, accounts, authentication, equipment, support-worker arrangements and a contact for failures. Test the actual workflow, not just whether a licence has been bought. Supply concise written steps, examples and an accessible induction timetable.

Offer a conversation about communication, focus, breaks, training and working patterns to everyone. A person can discuss a barrier without naming a condition. Agree what the team needs to know about practical arrangements; do not send a diagnosis to colleagues to justify them. Provide accessible routes to HR, benefits, IT and a contact outside the manager.

Set an early check-in and review again after the person has experienced real work. Training may need extra practice time, a sandbox, text instructions or demonstration. Ask which combination helps; a recording or long manual alone may be inadequate. Resolve access before treating incomplete induction as poor engagement.

**Boundary:** Do not wait for a passport, diagnosis or external funding before arranging ordinary practical support.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Use an optional confidential passport

<a id="passport"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-ONB-002 · **Action owner:** HR

**Intended outcome:** Agreed support survives transitions without unnecessary repeated disclosure.

Offer a short passport stating functional barriers, useful arrangements, owners, review triggers and agreed sharing. The employee should be able to check and correct it. Store it only in the employer's approved confidential system, with access limited to roles that need the information. The blank template in this knowledge collection must never be completed with real data on shared knowledge files.

At a manager or role change, plan a private handover with the person. Confirm which arrangements continue, which need checking in the new context and who owns implementation. Do not automatically withdraw effective support or require the person to restart every request. Keep the person informed of any proposed changes and supply interim access.

A passport is a support tool, not a waiver of rights or permission to collect a medical history. Portability means useful continuity within agreed boundaries; it does not authorise transfer to another employer or broad access to health information.

**Boundary:** Agreement about practical sharing does not by itself establish a lawful basis for all health-data processing.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [L7](../sources/source-register.md#l7).
