# Systems, benefits and procurement

**Status:** Draft practice guidance, knowledge edition 1.0. Organisational practices are proposed until adopted. Legal questions are starting points for appropriately qualified advice, not legal determinations.

## Respond to an inaccessible system or benefit

<a id="barrier-response"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-SYS-001 · **Action owner:** service owner

**Intended outcome:** People can use essential services with confidentiality and timely access.

Include payroll, HR, intranet, learning, employee assistance, mental-health support, benefits, collaboration, expenses and authentication in the service inventory. Ask whether people can achieve the real goal, not just open the application. An inaccessible benefit can exclude someone from support offered to colleagues.

Capture the goal, failed step, configuration, interaction method and practical impact with minimum necessary personal information. Reproduce with synthetic data. Put immediate access in place without unnecessarily asking a colleague to handle confidential financial or health information. Give a named owner and update time; do not require the person to prove the defect repeatedly.

Route internal defects to the product or engineering owner, supplier defects through procurement/vendor management, and benefits failures to the commissioning HR owner. Use the system-barrier workflow. Treat a discrimination concern seriously while leaving legal conclusions and remedies to qualified review of the facts.

**Boundary:** Do not assert automatic discrimination, personal liability or supplier breach from the defect alone.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Evaluate tasks and cumulative burden

<a id="task-analysis"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-SYS-002 · **Action owner:** accessibility lead

**Intended outcome:** Evidence identifies system friction without ranking employees.

Describe the goal, tasks, subtasks, alternatives and recovery routes. Observe representative use with voluntary, accessible participation, alongside what people report. Compare success, errors, steps, effort, fatigue, privacy, independence and time. Account for task familiarity, training, device, configuration and practice effects. Report uncertainty and use repeated observations where practical.

Time differences can reveal a system cost, but no employee should be raced against a colleague or punished for participation. A sighted mouse route is one comparator, not the universally correct standard. Do not import illustrative percentage tolerances from the source methodology as legal or universal acceptance thresholds. Evaluate criticality, frequency and the person's experience.

Estimate repeated burden across the day, including workarounds and recovery, and identify the design changes that would reduce it. Validate repairs against the full goal. A faster workaround that exposes private information or removes independent control is not an equivalent solution.

**Boundary:** The task-analysis method is adapted for employment safeguards; small samples do not establish population differences or individual capacity.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-10](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-10).

## Commission and remedy accessible services

<a id="supplier-remediation"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-SYS-003 · **Action owner:** procurement owner

**Intended outcome:** Supplier and internal commitments lead to verified fixes.

Before purchase, define representative employee goals, access needs, evidence required, testing, support routes and contract responsibilities. Examine the actual product version and end-to-end workflow rather than accepting a declaration or badge as proof. Include implementation, training, accessible support, updates and exported content.

When a defect appears, preserve a reproducible account, contractual representations and impact. Ask the service owner and supplier for remediation options, dates and verification evidence. Procurement and legal determine relevant contractual remedies, payment decisions and communications; managers must not independently threaten payment refusal or present breach as established fact.

Keep an accessible interim service and monitor it until repair is verified. Include unresolved barriers at renewal and consider alternatives through accountable decision-makers. Prioritise by impact, essentiality, frequency and lack of a usable alternative; do not dismiss a severe barrier because few people have reported it.

**Boundary:** A contract claim is not an employment adjustment; pursue supplier remedies while maintaining access for staff.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).
