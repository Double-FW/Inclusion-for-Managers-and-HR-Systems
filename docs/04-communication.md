# Communication and collaboration

## More than one way to contribute

<a id="meeting-participation"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-COM-001 · **Action owner:** meeting organiser

**Intended outcome:** People can contribute without avoidable time pressure or disclosure.

Ask participants what combination of speech, sign, text and advance input works. For AAC users, protect time to complete a contribution and offer an accessible queue or typed route. Do not finish someone's sentences or assume that slow speech means slow thought. A contribution sent before or after a meeting can be as valuable as one made live.

Send the purpose, agenda, materials and decisions needed in advance. A bare “quick chat” request may create avoidable uncertainty; state the topic and whether preparation is needed. Allow a pause or clarification without requiring public explanation. Record actions, owners, dates and decisions in an accessible summary and offer a realistic response window.

Messaging tools can help, but rapid chat can create motor, literacy or attention barriers. Check the actual platform, client, device and task. Keep alternative participation routes and agree response expectations rather than assuming permanent availability.

**Boundary:** No platform or communication mode is accessible for every participant; speed is not a proxy for contribution.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-04](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-04).

## Arrange Deaf and hearing access individually

<a id="deaf-access"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-COM-002 · **Action owner:** meeting organiser

**Intended outcome:** Participants can follow the full discussion and respond.

Ask about the person's language and communication needs. Not every Deaf person signs, and not every hard-of-hearing person lipreads. Depending on preferences, arrange qualified interpretation, captions, hearing support, text or other provision. Check captions and technical arrangements in practice rather than assuming automatic transcription is accurate enough.

Provide quiet acoustics, good lighting and sightlines; agree seating and interpreter placement. Use one speaker at a time, identify speakers and make remote participants equally able to follow. Do not make the person choose between seeing content and seeing the communication support. Short typed exchanges can help when usable but do not replace full access to a complex discussion.

Respect religious dress, appearance and privacy while arranging an effective communication method. Agree any recording or transcription with appropriate privacy review. Optional sign-language learning can build understanding but is not an interpreter service, and a Deaf colleague must not be required to teach or perform.

**Boundary:** Ask and verify access; do not prescribe a seat, lipreading or signing from hearing status.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Clarity, processing and feedback

<a id="clear-instructions"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-COM-003 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** People know what is expected and can respond in a usable format.

Agree preferences for text, voice, demonstration, structured notes and check-ins. Give instructions with the goal, priority, action, owner and date, and confirm verbal changes in an accessible written form where helpful. Distinguish required action from background information. Avoid relying on metaphor, hints or institutional knowledge to communicate a concern.

Use consistent names and locations for information; make instructions easy to find again. Break complex work into meaningful milestones and show dependencies. Offer accessible text descriptions alongside visual planning boards. Provide feedback close enough to the event to be useful, with specific examples, a route to improve and time to respond.

Protect time for reflection rather than expecting immediate brainstorming or decisions. Brief predictable check-ins can help if agreed; a compulsory weekly stand-up is not the solution for everyone. Avoid flooding people with repeated notifications or forcing multiple duplicated reporting channels.

**Boundary:** Clear and direct feedback must remain respectful; it does not justify bluntness, infantilisation or unilateral monitoring.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-04](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-04).

## Use accessible information and shared spaces

<a id="documents-tools"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-COM-004 · **Action owner:** process owner

**Intended outcome:** Everyone can obtain the same information and act on it.

Check actual use of email, policies, PDFs, charts, slides, whiteboards and co-working tools. Use meaningful headings and links, logical reading order, readable text and equivalent information beyond colour or images. Provide chart findings and structured data as appropriate. Give text directions alongside maps. Keep alternatives current when information changes.

For a digital whiteboard, check navigation, reading and editing with the team's interaction methods. Agree an alternative way to contribute and receive changes if the board is inaccessible. Do not silently move decisions into an exclusive channel or require someone to depend on a colleague's private interpretation.

Raise defects with the service owner and procurement where a supplier is involved. Check support, authentication and exported documents as well as the main screen. A plain-text summary can provide immediate access while repair continues, but it must preserve decisions, actions and opportunities to contribute.

**Boundary:** A workaround is not closure until the person can complete the intended goal with acceptable privacy and effort.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [DFW-06](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-06), [DFW-08](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-08), [DFW-09](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-09), [DFW-14](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-14).
