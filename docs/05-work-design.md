## Work patterns and legitimate outcomes

<a id="flexibility"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-WRK-001 · **Action owner:** line manager

**Intended outcome:** Working arrangements support sustainable contribution and real job needs.

Discuss when, where and how tasks can be done, including shifts, start/finish times, breaks, travel, task sequencing and remote activities. Identify actual duties requiring presence or cover instead of treating a job title as a prohibition. Distinguish the statutory flexible-working route from a disability-related adjustment; HR should identify which duties apply.

Assess outputs and impact alongside necessary coverage, collaboration, safety, contracted hours and sustainable effort. Output-only targets can reproduce exclusion if inaccessible tools consume extra time. Consider the whole workload and recovery time. Give advance notice of rotas, software and departmental changes where possible, and a clear route for urgent changes.

Job crafting can change methods or task allocation with the individual, but should not stereotype their strengths, diminish their progression or leave colleagues carrying an unplanned burden. Review effectiveness with everyone affected without sharing private reasons for an adjustment.

**Boundary:** Flexibility is not unlimited and presence requirements are not automatically decisive; assess the particular work and support options.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief), [L2](../sources/source-register.md#l2).

## Physical and sensory access

<a id="physical-sensory"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-WRK-002 · **Action owner:** facilities owner

**Intended outcome:** People can use the workplace safely and comfortably enough to do their work.

Review arrival, entrances, toilets, lifts, desk access, lighting, acoustics, temperature, signage and emergency arrangements. Include the remote workstation and travel between work sites where relevant. Ask about the actual task and environment rather than ordering standard equipment from a diagnosis.

Options include a predictable desk, adjustable equipment, reduced glare, alternative lighting, quiet focus zones and headphones. Check whether a proposed solution introduces another barrier: headphones may affect alarms or conversation; voice input may conflict with quiet work; a quiet room may not have suitable access. Seek a combination of space, scheduling and technology that supports all affected people.

Agree a discreet pause or recovery plan in advance, including a safe handover in roles requiring continuous cover. The person should not need a public explanation every time symptoms fluctuate. Provide a usable route for urgent assistance and test emergency access with appropriate safety expertise.

**Boundary:** Equipment and quiet areas are options to evaluate, not guaranteed fixes or substitutes for safe staffing and accessible design.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).

## Train and change without avoidable disruption

<a id="change-training"></a>

**Guidance ID:** IE-WRK-003 · **Action owner:** process owner

**Intended outcome:** People retain access when systems, workplaces or responsibilities change.

Before a change, identify affected tasks, interaction methods and existing adjustments. Explain what will change, when and why; provide accessible training, practice and support. A sandbox, worked example, short text guide or paced demonstration may be more useful than a single large presentation. Allow time for learning without automatically treating it as lower performance.

Test the new version or setup with representative tasks and assistive technology before removing the old route. Plan temporary alternatives and a named escalation contact if launch creates a barrier. Verify authentication, documents and integrations; an accessible main application can still have an inaccessible sign-in or support process.

Review after introduction and at transitions between role, manager and site. Preserve effective arrangements until there is a considered alternative. Record problems and fixes as process learning using synthetic or appropriately anonymised information, not employee medical detail.

**Boundary:** Awareness training alone cannot compensate for inaccessible tools, inadequate time or missing implementation ownership.

**Evidence:** [BRIEF](../sources/source-register.md#brief).
