# Connecting the disabled employee lifecycle framework

**Status:** Editorial crosswalk for local consideration, 22 November 2026; not adopted employer policy, legal advice or evidence of employee outcomes.

The public [Disabled Employee Lifecycle repository](https://github.com/Double-FW/Disabled-Employee-Lifecycle) provides a six-phase view of employment as a continuous experience. This crosswalk is based on a full reading of its [main lifecycle guide](https://github.com/Double-FW/Disabled-Employee-Lifecycle/blob/09f550787d2d46100639abd1676c666e05f0a74a/Accessibility%20in%20the%20Employee%20Lifecycle.md) at revision `09f550787d2d46100639abd1676c666e05f0a74a`. It connects that perspective to this edition’s more detailed processes and decision routes; it does not replace them or import external agent instructions. Business benefits in the source are intended outcomes to investigate, not independently verified findings or guarantees. See [DFW-15](../sources/source-register.md#dfw-15) for the exact review and rights boundaries.

This is a many-to-many relationship, not a replacement classification. Absence and return ([DT-23](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-23)–[DT-25](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-25)), manager/role/site or system change ([DT-26](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-26)–[DT-28](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-28)) and restructuring/redeployment ([DT-29](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-29)–[DT-30](../Inclusive_Employment_Lifecycle_Decision_Trees_v1_0.md#dt-30)) can cross several phases. Keep all eleven local stages, the forty DT identifiers and the shared routes intact. People may return to an earlier stage; the lifecycle is not a compulsory linear funnel.

## Continuity at a transition

Use these questions with the affected person and the roles that can deliver support. They are prompts, not a new mandatory form or permission to collect case data:

1. What is changing, what outcome matters, and which effective arrangements should continue?
2. Which actual tools, information, environments, relationships and opportunities need checking in the new context?
3. What minimum information may be shared, with whom and for what purpose? Agreement about practical sharing does not by itself establish every legal basis or retention rule.
4. Who coordinates, who owns implementation, and who is receiving the handover? Leave unconfirmed names, dates and decisions unassigned.
5. What interim access and route for urgent or disputed matters are available while a change is being resolved?
6. What will demonstrate that real work is usable after the transition, when will the person review it, and how can the issue be reopened?

Use the [optional passport](../docs/03-onboarding.md#passport), [responsibilities and escalation](../processes/responsibilities-and-escalation.md) and [outcome evaluation](../methods/outcome-evaluation.md) where relevant. Do not require a passport or repeated diagnosis disclosure before providing ordinary practical support. Completed records belong in the organisation’s approved confidential system, never in this public knowledge repository or dataset.

## Manager capability and delivery ownership

The source treats manager capability as cross-cutting. Use [line-manager guidance](../roles/line-managers.md), [HR guidance](../roles/hr.md), [leaders and service owners](../roles/leaders-and-service-owners.md) and [everyday culture](../docs/09-culture.md#everyday-culture) to make that practical. Training should address recognising barriers, usable communication, confidential handovers, delivering agreed support and obtaining help. Training attendance alone does not establish capability or employee access.

A manager may coordinate without controlling a platform, supplier, facilities budget or formal HR decision. The owner with the relevant authority remains responsible for delivery. Do not move the burden of navigating organisational boundaries onto the employee or invent a configured escalation service.

## Exit, learning and optional alumni contact

Offer usable exit information, appropriate participation routes and voluntary feedback without assuming a person wants retention discussions or ongoing contact. Clarify access and account/asset arrangements through actual organisational owners. Preserve continuity of support through the agreed exit process rather than withdrawing it as soon as notice is given.

If an alumni service exists, review its accessibility and let the person choose whether and how to participate. Alumni engagement does not authorise copying health, passport, adjustment, grievance or performance information into a mailing or contact system. Contact and any retained records need their actual purpose, authority, access and review arrangements; do not promise deletion or retention periods this repository has not established.

Use [outcome measures](../data/outcome-measures.md) and [continuous improvement](../processes/continuous-improvement.md) to investigate patterns across stages. Keep reported experience, direct observations, estimates, proposed benefits and actual organisational decisions distinct. State populations, denominators and exclusions where rates are used, and consider re-identification risk. Increased applications, progression or retention are possible signals, not proof that this framework caused an improvement; lower complaints may reflect either improvement or a reporting barrier.
