# Privacy, choice and practical records

## Keep the purpose clear

Support records should help someone work, obtain a service or participate fairly. Start by asking what information is genuinely needed for that purpose. A functional account of a barrier and helpful arrangement is often more useful than a detailed medical history. Where relevant advice is needed, explain why and use an appropriate confidential route.

Agree what practical information colleagues need without disclosing the reason unnecessarily. An arrangement such as written action summaries can be explained as a working method. A person's agreement about sharing preferences is not automatically a lawful basis for every health-data use; take data-protection questions to the relevant professional.

## Records that support action

A proportionate working record identifies the task, barrier, intended outcome, interim access, agreed arrangement, owner, next update and review. It distinguishes what was observed, what the person reported, what is inferred and what remains unknown. It allows correction and explains the route to challenge a decision.

Keep actual records in approved confidential systems with appropriate access, retention and correction arrangements. Blank templates are optional prompts, not a demand to disclose or a prerequisite for assistance. A passport supports continuity with agreed sharing; it is not a public profile or automatic permission to transfer information to another employer.

## Learning without identifying people

Share process learning rather than personal details. Removing a name may not anonymise a unique role, condition, date or event. Review whether combinations of details identify someone, particularly in small teams. Use fully synthetic examples where possible.

Research and team problem-solving should provide genuine choice. Do not use accessibility needs as a reason for closer monitoring, medical inference or involuntary recording of assistive-technology sessions. Keep optional equality monitoring separate from support decisions. See [data ethics](../docs/11-evaluation.md#data-ethics) and [legal starting questions](../legal-questions/privacy-ai-and-procurement.md).

## When privacy fails

Reduce further exposure and use the organisation's established security/privacy reporting route. The responsible specialists should assess containment, records and any legal duties. Deleting a visible copy may not remove other copies. Do not publish incident details in shared learning material while trying to get help.
