# Opportunities and best-practice approaches

An option is useful when it addresses a real barrier in context and supports the person's choices. These are ideas to explore, not guaranteed remedies, medical prescriptions or promises of cost, funding or retention effects.

| Barrier or opportunity | Approach to explore | Conditions for useful practice | Outcome to review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unclear work requirements | Replace vague traits with explicit tasks and outcomes | Retain genuinely essential requirements; assess relevant evidence | More accurate and fair assessment |
| Pressure in live interviews | Advance questions, work samples and usable response methods | Equivalent evidence standard; justified exceptions; appropriate payment for productive trials | Capability becomes visible |
| Rapid meeting discussion | Protected turns, AAC time and advance/later input | Actual tools and response windows work for participants | Equal influence on decisions |
| Hearing/language barriers | Chosen captioning, interpretation, text or hearing support | Individual preferences, tested quality and full discussion access | Understanding and contribution |
| Memory and uncertainty | Durable summaries, predictable routines and findable instructions | Clear priorities and manageable notifications | Confidence and fewer avoidable errors |
| Sensory overload | Quiet spaces, predictable desks and suitable equipment | Individual choice, safe alarms and access for competing needs | Sustainable effort and focus |
| Rigid timing | Task sequencing, breaks, rota or location options | Real coverage, safety and contractual questions addressed | Workable participation |
| Skill and aspiration mismatch | Collaborative job crafting and development | Avoid stereotypes, narrow careers and unfunded workload transfer | Contribution and progression |
| Inaccessible internal software | Task-based repair and representative retesting | Include authentication, support and exports | Independent end-to-end access |
| Inaccessible supplier services | Service ownership, procurement escalation and verified remedies | Maintain interim access; use authorised contract decisions | Durable service improvement |
| Confidential benefits blocked | An accessible private route to the same support | Avoid reliance on colleagues seeing health or financial details | Dignity and timely support |
| Manager change | Optional functional passport and agreed handover | Restricted sharing and continuity of effective support | Less repeated negotiation |
| Exhausting or ineffective workarounds | Bounded experiments with permanent ownership | Choice, privacy, stop route and realistic review | Useful learning and lower burden |
| AI opportunities | Person-chosen support for a bounded task | Approved service, minimum data, verification and non-AI option | Greater control with acceptable effort |
| Progression depends on social visibility | Published criteria and accessible opportunity allocation | Resource genuine specialist and management routes | Fair development and reward |
| Organisational learning is fragmented | Champions, networks and process learning | Protected time, sponsorship and accountable owners | Reusable improvements |
| Evaluation focuses on activity | Outcome measures and lived-experience review | Necessary data, clear limits and non-identifying reporting | Credible inclusion decisions |
| People fear raising barriers | Clear alternative reporting and review routes | Accessible response, confidentiality and fair action | Confidence and earlier resolution |

“This is how we have always done it” explains history, not whether a process works. Review the goal and actual constraint, involve the people affected and try proportionate alternatives.
