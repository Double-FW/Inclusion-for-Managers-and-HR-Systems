# Leaders and service owners

Managers can coordinate a response only when people with authority and resources resolve the parts they own. Leadership should make this practical: clear ownership, an accessible request route, interim support, resources and escalation for unresolved exclusion.

## What each function contributes

| Function | Practical contribution | Evidence of a useful outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Executive leadership | Priorities, resources, authority and challenge of persistent gaps | Essential access is restored and recurring causes addressed |
| Product and IT owners | Repair internal tools, authentication, integrations and support | People complete representative goals using their interaction methods |
| Procurement and vendor management | Evaluate evidence, obtain supplier remedies and review renewal options | Repair is independently verified; interim service remains usable |
| Facilities and safety owners | Physical, sensory, ergonomic, site and emergency access | People can arrive, work, move and respond to emergencies appropriately |
| HR service commissioners | Access to benefits, learning, employee assistance and confidential support | The full service is reachable without exposing private information |
| Privacy and security owners | Necessary collection, secure handling and proportionate permissions | Support and evaluation do not create intrusive staff profiles |
| Champions and networks | Local learning, practical advice and feedback | Teams improve processes with allocated time and owner follow-through |

## Questions for review

Which essential goal remains blocked? Who can change the relevant process? Is the interim route timely and confidential? What evidence will demonstrate repair? Are recurring barriers reaching budget and service decisions, rather than being left as personal workarounds?

Champions, staff networks and the affected employee are not substitutes for accountable service owners. Contract and payment decisions belong with authorised procurement/legal roles.
