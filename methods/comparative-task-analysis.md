# Comparative task analysis for system barriers

## Purpose

Understand the work required to achieve a goal and identify avoidable system burden. The subject is the process or system, not an employee's worth or a competition between people.

1. Define a meaningful goal, task sequence, alternatives and recovery routes.
2. Identify the actual setting: product version, device, interaction method, assistive technology, experience, training and task familiarity.
3. Arrange voluntary accessible observation alongside the person's account. Explain the purpose, information collected and lack of performance consequences.
4. Capture completion, errors, steps, assistance, effort, fatigue, privacy and time where useful. Repeated observations can reveal variability; tiny samples need explicit limits.
5. Locate the system decisions causing extra work and consider their frequency across the day.
6. Repair the design and retest the full goal under described conditions.

## Comparisons that help

A before/after comparison using a specified interaction method may reveal whether a repair reduces burden. Comparisons across methods need context rather than treating mouse use as a universally correct baseline. An assisted route can appear fast while removing privacy or independent control.

## Interpreting timing

Do not import illustrative percentage bands as law or universal acceptable delays. A small repeated delay, severe fatigue or an occasional block to essential pay may matter more than one large number. Distinguish measured observations from extrapolated workload estimates. See the [synthetic task data](../data/synthetic-task-observations.md).
