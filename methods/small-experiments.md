# Small experiments and useful workarounds

## Frame a bounded question

Ask whether a specific change helps a specific task in a defined context. Examples include an agenda with advance questions, a quieter desk, an accessible text contribution route or an optional AI summary. Avoid beginning with a product and looking for a person to use it.

Agree the intended outcome, owner's action, trial period, resources, privacy boundaries and how to stop or change the approach. Maintain an effective alternative while the trial runs. Do not make the employee absorb the cost of a failed experiment or repeatedly prove the barrier.

## Learn from success and failure

Review actual contribution, effort, privacy, confidence and sustainability. An apparent improvement in one dimension can conceal a problem in another: a buddy may speed completion but expose private information; a summariser may reduce reading but omit a decision. A failed trial is information about the response, not evidence of unwillingness.

## Keep permanent repair in view

Some workarounds are useful immediate support; others become a burden if maintained indefinitely. Name the underlying process owner, plan a durable fix where needed and preserve individual choice. Share only non-identifying process learning with appropriate permission.

**Boundary:** Radical or unfamiliar ideas can be worth exploring when proportionate, chosen and safe. Novelty is not proof of effectiveness. [Team solutions](../workflows/team-solutions.md) and [AI trials](../workflows/ai-trials.md).
