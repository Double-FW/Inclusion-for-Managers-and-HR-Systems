# Evaluate equitable and sustainable outcomes

## Begin with the intended experience

State what someone needs to achieve, understand, influence or choose. Examine completion alongside independence, effort, fatigue, timeliness, accuracy, dignity, privacy, confidence and satisfaction. Comparable experiences can use different routes and need not take identical time.

## Combine evidence

Use relevant task observations, operational records, accessible feedback and lived-experience input. Explain what was measured, who took part, the context and what remains unknown. Do not use survey or timing numbers as self-explanatory proof. Individual preferences can change, and an arrangement that worked previously may fail after a system or role change.

## Interpret organisational signals carefully

Define denominators and time windows for adjustment response, unresolved barriers, benefit access, progression and retention. Lower complaint counts may mean less confidence in reporting. Departures can have many causes. A completed training programme or inclusion scheme describes an activity or commitment; examine actual experience.

## Decide the next action

Record whether the arrangement should continue, change, be replaced or remain under investigation, with an owner and next update. Reopen if the goal remains blocked. Use [measure definitions](../data/outcome-measures.md) and [synthetic examples](../data/synthetic-task-observations.md) to practise interpretation without collecting real case data.

**Boundary:** No universal outcome score settles legal compliance or every person's experience. Data collection should be necessary, proportionate and privacy-preserving.
