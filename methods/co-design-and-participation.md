# Co-design and lived-experience participation

## Ask who should influence the work

Include people affected by a proposed change in understanding needs, choosing priorities, developing options and evaluating results. Disabled, Deaf and neurodivergent contributors bring relevant experience, but no one person represents every identity or access need.

Offer usable materials, communication support, preparation and contribution routes. Compensate commissioned expertise appropriately, protect employee time and explain how decisions will be made. Avoid relying on the same small group for every unpaid task.

## Protect choice and power

Participation should not require disclosure of a diagnosis or a public employment story. Make the purpose and sharing boundaries clear. Offer alternatives where a manager's involvement could inhibit honest feedback, and avoid connecting research participation to performance, benefits or promotion. An employee's agreement to participate is not automatically a lawful basis for every data use.

## Show what changed

Report the options considered, input received, decisions, reasons, remaining disagreements and next review. Consultation without influence is weak evidence of inclusion. Where needs conflict, explore combinations and trade-offs with affected people rather than choosing one group as more deserving.

**Boundary:** Research or co-design does not replace prompt repair of a known barrier, specialist legal advice or the organisation's responsibility to act. [Culture guidance](../docs/09-culture.md).
