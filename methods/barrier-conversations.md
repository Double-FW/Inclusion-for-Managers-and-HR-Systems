# A practical barrier conversation

## Start with the work

Ask what the person is trying to do, what gets in the way, what helps and what they would like to try. Offer a private usable format and enough time to respond. A diagnosis is not the starting point for ordinary practical support.

Separate the goal from the usual method. “Participate in a planning decision” does not necessarily mean “speak quickly in a live meeting”. “Record a defect clearly” does not automatically mean “present confidently to a large group”. Explore the actual requirement before choosing a solution.

## Explore without interrogating

Ask which tasks, conditions or times change the experience, what has already been tried and whether there are urgent effects on access, safety, pay or privacy. Invite preferences without making the person design the entire solution or justify their identity. Explain why any extra information or specialist advice is needed and how it will be handled.

## Turn the conversation into action

Agree interim support, the owner who can change the relevant process, the next update and how effectiveness will be reviewed. Ask what practical information may be shared. Provide a route outside the manager if needed. A written summary should be accessible and open to correction.

## Example

Instead of “What does your condition prevent you doing?”, ask “Which part of the weekly planning process is difficult to use, and how could you contribute most effectively?” A useful response might combine advance material, protected turn-taking and later written contributions. Check whether the person influenced the decision, not merely whether a message was sent.

**Boundary:** Do not promise a particular legal outcome or require unnecessary medical details. [Adjustment workflow](../workflows/adjustments.md).
