# AAC in a rapid meeting

**Type:** Synthetic worked scenario · **Reference:** SC-01 · **Related guidance:** IE-COM-001

## Situation and barrier

A team takes decisions during rapid spoken discussion. A colleague using typed AAC cannot secure a turn.

## Intended outcome

People can contribute without avoidable time pressure or disclosure.

## An approach to discuss

The chair agrees a visible accessible contribution queue, protects completion time, accepts advance/later input and publishes actions for comment.

## Responsibilities and process

The relevant coordinating role is meeting organiser. Involve the person affected and the owners able to change the process, technology or environment. Agree interim access, a next update and a route to review an ineffective response. Keep private details in the appropriate confidential system.

## Limits and alternatives

Do not infer cognitive capacity, finish contributions or require rapid chat.

## Questions for evaluating the outcome

The organiser and participant review whether contributions shaped the decision. Ask what changed in participation, effort, privacy, independence, confidence or opportunity, and what remains unresolved. An arrangement that improves one dimension may need modification if it creates another barrier.

## Discussion prompts

- What is the essential goal, and which habitual method is creating the barrier?
- What does the affected person want to try or avoid?
- Who can act now, and what requires specialist advice?
- What evidence would show this was useful in the real setting?
