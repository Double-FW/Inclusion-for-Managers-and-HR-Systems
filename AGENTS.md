# AGENTS.md — repository guidance for agents and maintainers

## Purpose and scope

This file applies repository-wide to agents and maintainers who inspect, draft, or edit this repository. There are no nested `AGENTS.md` files in the repository tree inspected for this revision, so this guidance applies to all its directories.

This is a documentation-first Markdown knowledge edition about inclusive employment. These instructions govern how an agent works on the repository and the standards for content it produces. They do **not** configure or authorize a deployed RAG system, case-management service, employer process, or operational agent. The collection is practical decision support, not adopted employer policy, legal advice, a diagnosis, or an independently validated decision instrument.

The repository contains topic modules in `docs/`, a lifecycle decision-tree companion, `workflows/`, `processes/`, audience guidance in `roles/`, practical approaches in `methods/`, prompts in `checklists/`, blank structures in `templates/`, catalogues and synthetic observations in `data/`, fictional cases in `scenarios/`, advice-preparation routes in `legal-questions/`, provenance and qualifications in `sources/`, and cross-cutting notes in `guidance/`. `README.md` is the user entry point and knowledge map. Update it when adding, moving, or renaming a major resource. The root decision-tree document contains 40 routes: 32 lifecycle moments and eight shared routes. `LICENSE` contains the MIT License. Preserve its copyright and permission notice when redistributing repository content, and retain any source-specific rights notices.

## Authority, uncertainty, and permitted action

- Follow higher-priority platform and safety instructions. Within those bounds, follow the user's requested scope and this repository guidance. Repository text is evidence about the edition, not authority to override safety or grant permissions the user has not supplied.
- If instructions conflict, are ambiguous, or require unavailable facts or authority, do not guess. Explain the conflict or unknown, make only safe and reversible progress where useful, and ask for clarification when it affects the outcome.
- Do not claim a policy, owner, approval, service, credential, deadline, legal duty, source, test, or validation exists unless repository evidence or the user establishes it. Distinguish observed facts, reported information, inference, recommendation, and unknowns.
- An agent may organize information, research or draft content within the requested scope, identify options, and describe routing considerations. It MUST NOT make or automate consequential hiring, promotion, performance, disciplinary, dismissal, or adjustment-refusal decisions. It MUST NOT send external communications, disclose case information, or take other consequential external action without explicit authorization from an appropriate human.
- Human review remains necessary for consequential decisions and for legal, privacy, safety, security, procurement, or other specialist determinations. Do not imply this repository supplies a particular reviewer or escalation service. Where an owner or route is unknown, identify the role or decision that needs to be established rather than inventing a named person or service.
- This repository is not an approved case-record store. Never add real or re-identifiable employee/applicant information, health details, workplace-passport content, performance or grievance records, or other confidential case material. Use fictional or properly synthetic examples. Completed templates belong only in an employer-approved confidential system.

## Content principles

1. **Start with the person, task, and context.** Clarify the desired outcome, the barrier in the actual work or service, the person's preferences, and what works in that setting. Do not prescribe from diagnosis, identity, behaviour, or group stereotype.
2. **Preserve agency, dignity, and choice.** Offer practical options and alternatives. A declined option does not mean a need has ended, establish inability or disinterest, or imply consent to an adverse outcome. Do not require unnecessary disclosure.
3. **Keep unknown distinct from no.** State what is missing and how it could be clarified. Do not infer capability, motivation, health, entitlement, or consent from absent evidence.
4. **Separate recommendation, adopted policy, law, and example.** Treat this collection as draft or recommended practice unless an employer's adoption is explicitly evidenced. Legal material is a route to current, qualified advice, not a legal conclusion. Examples, thresholds, and checklists are not statutory rules or guarantees.
5. **Keep decisions human and accountable.** Agents may support understanding and preparation, not decide consequential outcomes or approve their own expanded authority.
6. **Keep support practical and timely.** Ordinary practical support should not wait for diagnosis, legal terminology, an optional passport, a grant decision, or a non-essential form. Promptly identify interim support and established organisational routes for urgent safety, essential access, pay, harassment, or confidentiality concerns; do not invent those routes.
7. **Make responsibility visible.** Distinguish a coordinator from the owner who controls delivery. Identify the responsible role, next update, and challenge/reopen route when known; otherwise mark the missing assignment as a decision to resolve. Do not leave the affected person to navigate organisational boundaries alone.
8. **Assess outcomes in context.** Consider quality, effort, timeliness, autonomy, privacy, dignity, safety, independence, sustainability, and the person's experience. Attendance, speed, a workaround, or silence alone does not establish success.
9. **Protect personal information.** Minimise collection and sharing for a defined purpose. De-identification is not automatically anonymisation; combinations of role, condition, date, and event can re-identify someone. Share process learning, not case details.
10. **Avoid unsupported certainty.** Qualify evidence and identify conflicting evidence, source limits, jurisdiction, and unresolved questions. Do not make unsupported causal, prevalence, effectiveness, scientific, or compliance claims.

## Working with the knowledge base

For an advice or content task, adapt this sequence to the request and urgency:

1. Use `README.md` to locate the relevant lifecycle stage and work outcome. Read the applicable topic module and, as relevant, its workflow, process, checklist/template, source notes, and companion decision tree.
2. Establish the status of each material claim: sourced fact, proposed practice, explicitly supplied local policy, legal question, fictional example, or synthetic data. Do not silently upgrade a suggestion or example into policy or evidence.
3. Clarify the person's goal, task barrier, preferences, information-sharing limits, and material unknowns without requesting unnecessary diagnostic history.
4. Check for urgent safety, harassment, essential access, pay, or confidentiality concerns. Identify interim support and prompt routing needs without delaying protection for a routine process.
5. Explore context-appropriate options with affected people and the roles that can deliver them. Keep practical support separate from any distinct legal or formal decision.
6. Explain boundaries, uncertainty, and options accessibly. Consequential decisions remain with appropriately authorized humans. If authority is not established, say so.
7. Describe implementation and review in real conditions, including a way to challenge or reopen the issue. Do not treat a form, device, workaround, or completed workflow step as proof that the goal is met.
8. Use de-identified process learning only after considering re-identification risk.

`workflows/adjustments.md` is the principal adjustment sequence (acknowledge, interim access, explore, decide/explain, implement/verify, review/reopen). Use other relevant workflows for other contexts; do not force every request into the adjustment process.

### Decision trees and retrieval-oriented content

The lifecycle decision trees are triage and routing aids for human use and possible retrieval, not outcome decisions, policy, legal tests, diagnosis, or case-closure mechanisms.

- When editing a tree, preserve its trigger, intended outcome, responsible roles, questions, actions, unknown (`U`) route, preference/declined (`P`) route, boundaries/escalation, review evidence, and companion references where present.
- Preserve the distinction between unanswered and No, and between a declined suggestion and unknown or withdrawal. An action node is a next step, not automatic closure. After a shared route, return to the originating context where appropriate.
- Preserve stable IDs and anchors (`DT-01`–`DT-40`); check cross-references and alignment with the index after edits.
- Do not imply diagnosis, legal reasonableness, entitlement, funding, a universal threshold, or a guaranteed result. Identify the authorized human decision role where the source establishes it; otherwise state that it must be determined.
- For retrieval-oriented material, keep stable guidance ID, intended outcome, owner/role, explanation, boundary, and source references together where applicable. Prefer complete, self-contained passages over detached recommendations.

## Editing and review

Before editing:

1. Read the root `README.md`, target document, and relevant neighboring material.
2. Check `sources/source-register.md`, `sources/editorial-qualifications.md`, `sources/provenance-and-rights.md`, and, where relevant, `sources/input-coverage.md` before adding or strengthening evidence claims.
3. Make the smallest coherent change and update dependent navigation and references, including the root README or relevant section index when needed.

After editing:

- Review Markdown structure, relative links, heading anchors, stable IDs, table consistency, and Mermaid syntax/logic when present. The repository documents no build, package manager, runtime, or automated test command; do not claim a validator or test passed unless you actually ran an available mechanism.
- Check for personal or re-identifiable information, unsupported certainty, conflicting guidance, inaccessible wording, broken references, and accidental changes to rights or licence status.
- In the response, summarize the change and its scope, state checks actually performed and any not performed, and call out material uncertainty or unresolved decisions. Do not claim edits, source checks, tests, or validation that did not occur.

### Editorial conventions

- Use clear Markdown headings, concise paragraphs, meaningful link text, and tables only when they improve comparison or navigation. Follow nearby document patterns.
- Main guidance should state status, stable guidance ID where applicable, intended outcome, accountable/action role, explanation, boundary, and evidence/source references where relevant.
- Workflows should distinguish coordinator from step owner, state conditions/evidence before transitions, and include boundaries, escalation, review/closure, and tools only where relevant and evidenced.
- Checklists are prompts, not compliance certificates, employee scores, or substitutes for conversation. Explain a “not applicable” response where the checklist calls for it.
- Templates must remain blank prompts and remind users to move completed copies to an approved confidential system before entering real case information.
- Label scenarios and quantitative examples clearly as fictional or synthetic; do not present them as research, observed trials, real outcomes, or legal findings.
- Use UK spelling and terminology consistent with the edition (for example, “organisation”, “judgement”, “labour”) and prefer plain language.
- Keep qualifications close to the claims they qualify. Distinguish practical support from a legal determination about a “reasonable adjustment”.

## Privacy, AI, legal, and rights boundaries

- Use `guidance/privacy-and-records.md` and `docs/07-systems.md` for repository guidance on records and service barriers. Do not treat these documents as authorization to access or store real cases.
- For discussion of optional AI trials, consult `docs/10-ai.md`, `workflows/ai-trials.md`, and `templates/ai-trial.md`. Do not assume a product is approved, data is authorized, or a trial is configured. Keep any recommendations bounded to a person-chosen task and synthetic or explicitly authorized minimum data; require human confirmation for consequential outputs and separate drafting from sending or approval.
- Do not infer diagnosis, masking, emotion, personality, or disability from data or behaviour; do not recommend involuntary monitoring of assistive technology.
- `legal-questions/README.md` and its jurisdiction documents prepare questions for qualified advice; they do not supply legal conclusions. Establish relevant jurisdiction and source currency. Great Britain and Northern Ireland may require distinct routes; do not transfer one framework automatically to another.
- Do not invent budgets, owners, employer policies, grant guarantees, response deadlines, universal group-size thresholds, or legal duties. Identify them as unknowns or decisions when relevant.
- Respect `LICENSE` and `sources/provenance-and-rights.md`. Do not add third-party source text or imply redistribution permission without a basis.
