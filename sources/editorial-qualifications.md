# Editorial qualifications and interpretation

The collection preserves the practical aim of removing barriers while avoiding unsupported certainty.

| Idea | Qualification used here |
| --- | --- |
| Support from a diagnosis | Ask about the individual, task and context; diagnostic groups do not prescribe one solution |
| Flexibility by occupation | Examine actual duties and applicable legal routes rather than excluding whole occupations |
| Output over hours | Include sustainable effort, necessary coverage, safety and contractual constraints |
| Grants remove employer cost | Additional schemes do not replace the employer’s responsibilities; funding is not guaranteed |
| No medical information can ever be relevant | Avoid unnecessary diagnosis requirements; explain any proportionate functional advice needed |
| Disability absence is always exempt | Consider relevant arrangements individually with appropriate advice |
| All psychometrics are fake science | Avoid the unsupported absolute; prefer relevant accessible evidence and exclude personality conformity from recommendations |
| Personality activities are harmless fun | Participation, privacy and consequences still matter |
| A fixed preparation period is law | Five working days is an example of a proposed practice default, not a statutory period |
| Anonymisation eliminates bias | It may reduce cues while criteria, scoring and proxies still need review |
| Defects establish discrimination or breach | Provide access and gather facts; legal and contractual conclusions require appropriate assessment |
| Buddying equals inclusion | Chosen assistance can help, but independent access, privacy and status still matter |
| A Deaf person needs a particular communication method | Ask language and access preferences; signing, captions, text and hearing support are distinct |
| Timed comparison establishes employee capacity | Evaluate systems and cumulative burden with context, choice and uncertainty |
| AI fixes accessibility | Trial a defined use with verification, privacy, choice and a non-AI route |
| An inclusion scheme or training proves outcomes | Examine actual access, opportunity, experience and sustained improvement |
| A mnemonic is an established evidence-based method | Treat the supplied SHAPE wording as an unverified organising aid only |

The source review is bounded: twelve READMEs and six relevant detailed documents were retrieved during preparation; five requested collections remained unavailable. Several statutory checks used limited excerpts. Current law, applicability, staff experience and implementation outcomes have not been independently established by this editorial work.
