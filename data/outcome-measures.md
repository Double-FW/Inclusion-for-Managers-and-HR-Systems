# Outcome measures and interpretation

These definitions are options for purposeful evaluation, not a mandated scorecard. Choose the smallest set that helps a real decision, with appropriate privacy review. None is a universal legal threshold.

| Measure | What it describes | Interpretation and context | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time to useful acknowledgement | Time from a request to a response with a named owner and next step | Describe the start/end events and report variation | A generic automated receipt does not show useful support |
| Time to interim access | Time until the essential goal has a workable temporary route | Check privacy and actual goal completion as well as elapsed time | Speed alone can conceal an undignified workaround |
| Time to implemented support | Time from identified need to an arrangement actually working | Record dependencies and unresolved cases separately | Approval or purchase is not implementation |
| Effectiveness of adjustments | Count of reviewed arrangements the person reports as useful, divided by arrangements reviewed | Report response rate, criteria and time window | Not the proportion of all staff whose needs are met |
| Unresolved essential barriers | Count of known essential goals still blocked at the review point | Include age, impact and owner rather than only total count | Few reports may reflect inaccessible reporting |
| Recurrence | Previously repaired barriers that reappear in a defined period | Explain service changes and how recurrence is detected | A count without exposure/context can mislead |
| Task completion | Completed intended goals divided by attempts in the defined exercise | Describe tasks, context, assistance and exclusions | Completion does not prove independence or comparable effort |
| Task effort and fatigue | Person’s account and observed steps/recovery needed for a specified goal | Use a clear local description and preserve individual variation | An arbitrary rating scale is not a medical or legal threshold |
| Timeliness of a service | Whether a usable route delivers the needed result when it matters | Define the actual deadline or service need | Average speed may conceal a critical blocked case |
| Confidential service access | Whether someone can use a benefit without unnecessary disclosure to intermediaries | Examine the entire route including authentication and support | A colleague completing a task may remove privacy |
| Learning and progression access | Opportunity offered, take-up and progression within a defined eligible group | Explain eligibility, working patterns, selection and missingness | Differences do not alone establish cause or discrimination |
| Satisfaction and confidence | Voluntary accessible feedback about a defined experience | State question, response options, respondents and non-response | Do not infer all staff experience from a small self-selected sample |
| Confidence in raising concerns | Whether people expect a usable, fair response without retaliation | Offer confidential routes and explain sharing limits | A lower complaint count is not necessarily improvement |
| Retention and exit | People remaining or leaving in a defined cohort and interval | Clarify denominator, period and voluntarily supplied context | Retention does not prove inclusion; departure does not prove exclusion |

## Responsible use

Separate support records from optional equality monitoring. Define lawful purpose, necessary data, access, retention and correction routes before collection. Review small-group disclosure with privacy expertise; do not invent a universally safe minimum group size. Do not infer disability from settings or assistive-technology use. Measures should inform removal of barriers, not rank disabled workers or reward managers for lower disclosure.
