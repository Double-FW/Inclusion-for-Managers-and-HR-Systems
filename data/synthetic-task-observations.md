# Synthetic task observations: practising interpretation

**All values, situations and comments below are invented.** They are an arithmetic and reasoning example, not research, an employee benchmark, a service claim or evidence that a particular repair works. The task conditions are simplified for explanation.

## Fictional before-and-after task values

Assume the same described interaction method, familiar task and configuration apart from the stated repair. These assumptions would need testing in a real evaluation. Times are illustrative single values; they do not express measurement precision or population averages.

| Fictional task | Interaction context | Proposed repair | Before (seconds) | After (seconds) | Difference (seconds) |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Submit an expense claim | Keyboard and screen reader | Repair labels, focus order and error recovery | 360 | 210 | 150 |
| Request leave | Keyboard only | Remove a keyboard trap and clarify status | 180 | 120 | 60 |
| Retrieve a report finding | Magnification and text | Add a usable summary and structured data | 480 | 240 | 240 |

The difference is the before value minus the after value. A smaller time is only one dimension. Check accuracy, completion, privacy, independence, effort and confidence. No comparison with a “fastest employee” is involved.

## Fictional cumulative-workload estimate

For illustration only, assume a workload containing all three tasks at the stated daily frequencies. Real frequencies, task variation, recovery and interruptions would need evidence.

| Task | Illustrative repetitions per day | Difference per task (seconds) | Estimated daily difference (seconds) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Expense claim | 4 | 150 | 600 |
| Leave request | 2 | 60 | 120 |
| Report finding | 3 | 240 | 720 |
| Total | — | — | 1,440 |

The illustrative total is 24 minutes per day. It is an estimate from invented frequencies and values, not a measured productivity gain or an individual capacity score. Do not extrapolate it into financial savings, staffing cuts or population claims.

## Conflicting outcome examples

| Fictional response | Task result | Other experience | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A colleague completes a wellbeing form for someone | Form submitted | Private information is exposed to the colleague | Completion alone does not establish equivalent confidential access |
| An AI tool produces a short summary quickly | Summary delivered | A key decision is omitted | Apparent speed does not establish useful support |
| A meeting allows written contributions after discussion | Contribution received | The decision has already become irreversible | A route exists, but the opportunity to influence is weak |
| A phased return keeps a full workload | Targets temporarily met | Recovery is unsustainable | Output does not establish a workable arrangement |

## Questions for a real evaluation

Who chose the task and comparison? Were participants free to decline? What experience, training or configuration differences matter? How variable are the results? What assistance and recovery are hidden? Does the change preserve choice and privacy? What can the data reasonably support, and what remains unknown?
