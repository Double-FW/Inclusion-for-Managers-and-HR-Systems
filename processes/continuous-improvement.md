# Continuous improvement through real outcomes

Begin with a barrier or a question about participation. Combine accessible defaults, individual options and system repair rather than repeatedly treating each person as an exception.

## A practical cycle

1. **Understand:** identify the goal, context and people affected. Distinguish observed difficulty from assumptions about its cause.
2. **Stabilise:** provide timely interim access, safe cover and appropriate privacy.
3. **Explore:** consider process, tool, information, environment and work-design options with affected people.
4. **Try:** make a proportionate change with an owner, review point and route to stop or reverse it.
5. **Evaluate:** examine participation, effort, accuracy, confidence, dignity, satisfaction and sustainability.
6. **Learn:** repair recurring causes, retain useful support and share non-identifying process knowledge.

This cycle is a practical organising approach, not a validated clinical or legal model. A supplied SHAPE mnemonic may describe similar activity, but no established status is asserted for it. “Hold”, if used, means stabilise support; it never means delay urgent action.

## What organisational learning needs

Ask whether the same barriers appear in different teams or stages. Connect findings to commissioning, training, premises, work allocation and leadership decisions. Give champions time and resources to help spread learning while service owners retain responsibility.

Use [outcome measures](../data/outcome-measures.md) with clear denominators and limitations. A reduction in reported concerns may reflect improvement, fear or an unusable reporting route. A completed initiative should lead to a better experience, not just a higher activity count.
