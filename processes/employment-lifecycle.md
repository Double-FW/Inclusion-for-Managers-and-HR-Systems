# Inclusion across the employment lifecycle

An accessible hiring process is only the beginning. At every transition, ask what information, tools, relationships, environments and opportunities will change, and whether effective support will continue.

| Stage | Common exclusion risk | Practical approach | Outcome to examine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Attraction and role design | Boilerplate requirements deter suitable applicants | Specify real tasks, available patterns and usable access contact | People can judge the opportunity and apply |
| Application and assessment | Inaccessible systems or conversational pressure obscure competence | Offer usable preparation and relevant alternative evidence | Capability is assessed fairly |
| Offer and pre-start | Equipment and support are deferred until arrival | Confirm access and implementation ownership in advance | The person starts with workable arrangements |
| Induction and probation | Fast training, unclear priorities or inaccessible tools appear as poor engagement | Provide practice, clear expectations and early review | The person can learn and show relevant work |
| Daily work and benefits | Informal decisions or inaccessible services exclude staff | Agree communication routes; test complete services | People contribute and obtain support privately |
| Learning and reward | Visibility and networking substitute for evidence | Check access to development, feedback and reward criteria | Opportunities reflect contribution and aspiration |
| Promotion and progression | A single career model rewards social conformity | Publish work criteria and supported specialist/management routes | People can advance without identity performance |
| Absence and return | Standard triggers or compressed workloads disregard circumstances | Discuss individual arrangements and sustainable return | Recovery and work participation are supported |
| Manager, role or site change | Existing support disappears at handover | Agree continuity and what needs retesting | The person does not restart every request |
| Restructuring or redeployment | Inaccessible consultation or assumptions remove options | Provide usable information and assess actual roles with support | Choices and concerns can be understood and challenged |
| Leaving or retirement | Inaccessible exit processes or involuntary health disclosure | Offer usable information, return/access arrangements and optional feedback | Exit is respectful and learning is proportionate |

## Continuity conversation

Before a transition, ask which arrangements work, what will change, what information may be shared and who will maintain support. Explain any proposed changes and review them with the person. Do not automatically remove an effective adjustment because a manager, budget code or platform changes.

## Learning across stages

Review whether barriers recur between stages. A successful interview followed by inaccessible benefits or learning is a lifecycle failure. Retention, departures and complaint counts are signals to explore, not proof of success or failure on their own. Use [outcome evaluation](../methods/outcome-evaluation.md).
