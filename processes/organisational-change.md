# Inclusion during organisational change

Changes in tools, reporting lines, premises, schedules and job structure can create new barriers or remove effective support. Plan access as part of the change rather than waiting for individual failures.

1. Identify affected goals, people and working arrangements. Include remote, hybrid, shift and frontline tasks, authentication, documents and support routes.
2. Ask affected people how they can contribute to planning without requiring unnecessary disclosure. Include lived-experience expertise and accessible consultation methods.
3. Explain the change, timing, preparation and genuine options. Give notice where possible and a clear route for urgent changes.
4. Test representative tasks and existing assistive-technology arrangements. A vendor statement or demonstration by an expert user is not enough to establish all staff access.
5. Provide usable training, practice time and interim routes. Agree who can pause rollout, restore access or authorise a different approach if essential work is blocked.
6. Review after introduction and at manager/role handover. Record recurrent causes and changes needed to commissioning or change management.

Do not assume support can be withdrawn automatically during restructuring. Where a decision affects employment rights or removes opportunities, use appropriate HR/legal advice and an accessible challenge route.
