# Responsibilities and escalation

The manager is often the coordinator, but the owner of the process, premises or technology needs to act on the barrier. The affected person informs the approach without carrying the full burden of escalation.

| Situation | Usual coordination | Owner or advice to involve | Immediate consideration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Communication or work-pattern barrier | Line manager | HR and relevant operational owners | Agree a usable interim way to work |
| Internal system failure | Service owner | Product, engineering, IT and accessibility expertise | Preserve task access and confidentiality |
| Supplier product failure | Service owner | Procurement/vendor management; legal for remedies | Maintain access while contractual work continues |
| Inaccessible benefit or wellbeing service | Commissioning HR owner | Service owner, provider, privacy | Avoid forcing disclosure to colleagues |
| Physical or emergency access | Facilities/safety owner | Affected person and relevant expertise | Appropriate safety and interim access |
| Disputed adjustment or performance decision | HR | Authorised decision-maker and qualified advice | A fair response and challenge route |
| Manager implicated in a concern | Independent organisational route | HR/employee relations, union or other relevant support | Confidential reporting and protection from retaliation |
| AI or data exposure | Relevant service owner | Privacy/security and authorised management | Stop harmful use and restore support |

## A useful handover

Describe the goal, observable barrier, practical effect, urgency, interim support and the change needed. Name the receiving owner, next update and a way to challenge or reopen the issue. Do not forward unnecessary medical information to make the issue seem serious.

Escalate the unresolved decision rather than circulating the person between teams. A funding disagreement should reach someone able to allocate resources. A supplier contract dispute should not suspend support for staff. Explain delays honestly and retain a usable route to essential work.

## What closure means

The intended goal can be achieved with acceptable effort, dignity, privacy and independence; continuing support and ownership are clear; remaining limitations are understood; and the person can seek review. A ticket marked closed, a training session or equipment order does not establish those outcomes.
